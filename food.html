Food and coffee by stop

Where to eat and drink

Three lists per stop: celiac-safe places for Lisa (safest first), vegetarian standouts for Kalindi, and third-wave coffee. Places marked Both work for Lisa and Kalindi.

Safety levels. Dedicated whole kitchen gluten-free. Careful shared kitchen, celiacs report good handling. Ask gluten-free dishes exist, cross-contamination unclear.

Checked 26 Nov 2026 (Niseko and Otaru rechecked the same night). Hours and closing days change, so confirm on Instagram or Google before going.

Tokyo · Ginza base

Tokyo

For her

  • FANCL Brown Rice Meals Dedicated Both

    Ginza 5-8-16, FANCL Ginza Square B1F. 1 min from Ginza Station exit A4.

    Every dish gluten-free, wheat kept out of equipment. Lunch 11:30 to 16:00; dinner 17:00 to 22:00 weekdays, 17:00 to 21:00 weekends. Closed one day a month. Weekday lunch vegan plate set (¥1,850); dinner has only one daily vegan option.

  • Gluten Free Kushiage Su Dedicated

    Ginza 8-15-2, Ginza Park Building 2F.

    Six-seat counter, reservation only, seasonal gluten-free omakase of fried skewers. Dinner 19:00 (all start together), ¥13,800 from October 2026. Sunday lunch 12:00, around ¥6,000. Closed Mondays. Book via TableCheck. Enter via intercom 201 at the side entrance. Vegetarian not confirmed (seafood-heavy; pescatarian offered).

  • Ain Soph Ginza Careful Both

    Higashi-Ginza, near Kabukiza.

    Vegan Japanese, gluten-free dishes and pancakes. Some cross-contamination risk noted by Atly. Book ahead.

  • Soranoiro Nippon Careful Both

    Tokyo Station, Ramen Street, about 1 km from Ginza.

    Gluten-free shio ramen with brown rice noodles, gluten-free gyoza. Vegan ramen overlaps with the gluten-free option. Expect a queue.

  • Tsukiji Tama Sushi Ask

    Tsukiji 2-15-19, walkable from Ginza.

    Allergens listed, no gluten-free soy sauce (Jan 2026 review), bringing your own is fine. Vegetarian limited.

  • Kushiyaki Bistro Fukumimi Ginza Ask

    Ginza 5-10-9, B1F.

    Crowd reviews only, least verified. 2-hour limit.

For you

  • Faro

    Fine dining that focuses on vegan menus; the vegan tasting menu needs an advance reservation. Kalindi's special-dinner pick. Michelin Guide

  • Ain Soph Ginza

    Fine plant-based Japanese; lunch 11:30 to 15:00, tea time to 17:00, dinner from 18:00. The dinner menu is the one reviewers rate highest.

Coffee

Kanazawa

Wed evening and Thu. The strongest food stop on the trip for both of you.

For her

    Dedicated gluten-free (apart from a couple of beers; gluten-free beer available), low cross-contamination risk on Atly. About 99% vegetarian and vegan, serves some fish. Probably your best shared dinner of the trip. Tasting menu at dinner. Hours can be unpredictable, check Instagram. Atly

    4-8 Oyamamachi.

    100% gluten-free galettes and crepes. Small, owner-run. Good lunch. Gluto

    Rice-flour pancakes reported celiac-safe. Breakfast or dessert.

  • Aashirwad Careful Both

    Between Omicho Market and Nagamachi.

    Atly lists low cross-contamination risk and trained staff; a celiac reported staff were very careful. Large vegetarian and vegan menu. Popular with locals, reserve. Atly

    Build-your-own hand rolls; has gluten-free soy sauce and can assemble safe sets. One celiac had to correct staff on a mayonnaise, and the set comes with wheat tempura (swap it). Fun, very Kanazawa. Vegan-friendly option.

  • Osushi to Genshiyaki Nakamura Ask

    Horikawacho, near Kanazawa Station.

    Izakaya that cooks with gluten-free in mind. Dinner 17:00 to 23:00. Closed Wednesdays (and some Tuesdays), so Thu 4 Feb only.

    Kakinokibatake.

    Long-established macrobiotic vegan Japanese, vegetables from its own farm, sprouted brown rice. Reviewers call it one of the best meals in Kanazawa. A bit outside the centre (Mitsukuchishinmachi).

    Small, many vegan and vegetarian dishes, English menu.

    Small Chinese-style place; ask for the separate vegetarian menu. Reviewers rave about the vegetable ramen.

  • SIU.COFFEE +VEG

    Fully vegan Mediterranean-style cafe. A walk from the main sights.

Coffee

    Takaokamachi, near Omicho Market. Light roasts by a barista trained in Denmark. The most consistently praised roaster in the city. Top pick.

  • Nonstop Coffee Stand & Roastery

    Near Higashi Chaya. Tiny stand, no seating; flat whites and hand drip served in wine glasses.

  • Curio Espresso and Vintage Design Cafe

    Yasuecho. One of the first new-wave shops in Kanazawa; good breakfast.

    Just outside Kanazawa Castle's north gate. Roastery, light-roast Geisha.

  • and KANAZAWA

    Higashi Chaya district. Cafe in a renovated house.

Everywhere in Japan

Tips from celiacs

  • Standard soy sauce is brewed with wheat. Dashi, marinades and some miso can contain wheat or barley.

  • Carry a Japanese allergy card (Legal Nomads or Equal Eats). Show the card before taking out your own gluten-free soy sauce packets, and ask permission.

  • Ask about shared fryers, shared cooking water and shared surfaces, not just "does it contain wheat". Tempura, tonkatsu and karaage often share fryers.

  • Many soba noodles contain wheat flour; only 100% buckwheat (juwari) soba is a candidate.

  • Book ahead; check opening hours on Instagram; carry cash.

  • Konbini food is backup only. Plain salted salmon or umeboshi (pickled plum) onigiri are the safer picks. Read the whole label, including the "made in a factory that handles" line.

  • Kanji to watch for

    小麦 (wheat), 大麦 (barley), 麦芽 (malt), 全粒小麦 (wholewheat), 小麦澱粉 (wheat starch), 醤油 (soy sauce), グルテン (gluten).

  • Use Google Translate's camera feature to read labels.

  • Walk away if staff can't clearly confirm.
